On June 22, 2026, the White House issued an executive order aimed at consolidating U.S. leadership across quantum information science and technology (QIST). The action frames quantum computing, sensing, and networking as strategic national priorities—and directs a coordinated, whole-of-government push to accelerate research, commercialization, and secure deployment.

## What the order covers

The executive order spans the full quantum innovation lifecycle:

- **National strategy refresh.** Within 180 days, senior science and national security leaders must update the National Quantum Strategy, with agency alignment reports due shortly after publication.
- **Large-scale quantum computing.** A new Quantum Computer for Application Development and Discovery Science (QC-ADDS) effort targets deployment of high-impact systems for scientific and national security use, supported by public–private partnership models and performance benchmarking centers.
- **Sensors and networks.** Agencies must prioritize next-generation quantum sensors for fielding by 2028 and develop five-year plans for quantum sensing, imaging, and networking applications.
- **Domestic supply chains.** Commerce, Energy, Defense, and NSF are tasked with strengthening QIST manufacturing, standards adoption, and access to critical quantum-enabling components.
- **Workforce and international engagement.** The order expands recruitment, training, and allied coordination to protect trusted quantum ecosystems while limiting adversarial access to sensitive technologies.

## Why post-quantum cryptography matters here

While much of the order focuses on accelerating quantum capability, it also embeds a clear security imperative. Section 4(f) directs intelligence and defense leaders—working with the ESIX subcommittee—to assess the **national security implications of increasingly capable commercial quantum computers**, explicitly including implications for **migration to post-quantum cryptography**.

That linkage is significant. Federal policy is no longer treating quantum innovation and cryptographic modernization as separate tracks. As quantum hardware matures, the window for proactive PQC transition narrows—and government expectations for risk assessment and readiness are rising in parallel.

## Implications for organizations

For enterprises, critical infrastructure operators, and technology vendors, this executive order reinforces several practical priorities:

1. **Treat PQC as a strategic program, not a research exercise.** Federal attention to commercial quantum scale makes crypto-agility and NIST-aligned migration planning a board-level resilience issue.
2. **Inventory long-lived cryptographic dependencies now.** Systems protecting data with multi-decade confidentiality requirements face the highest exposure as quantum timelines compress.
3. **Align roadmaps with emerging federal guidance.** Expect increased coordination across agencies on quantum security messaging, threat sharing, and industry outreach—including through expanded counterintelligence protection teams focused on the QIST ecosystem.
4. **Plan for hybrid and phased transitions.** Organizations that map dependencies, pilot PQC algorithms, and design interoperable migration paths will be best positioned as policy and procurement expectations evolve.
