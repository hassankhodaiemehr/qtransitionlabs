Under **Executive Order 14306** and the federal PQC program led with **NIST** and **NSA**, **CISA** maintains **[Product Categories for Technologies That Use Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/product-categories-technologies-use-post-quantum-cryptography-standards)**—a procurement-oriented map of where **NIST PQC standards** must appear in the U.S. government technology stack.

## Why a category list matters

OMB **M-26-15** tells agencies to ensure requirements for products in covered categories **include PQC integration**. Without a shared taxonomy, every agency reinvents vendor questionnaires; CISA’s list instead anchors:

- **What classes of products** are in scope for near-term PQC mandates  
- **Which widely available product types** are expected to adopt **ML-KEM**, **ML-DSA**, and **SLH-DSA**  
- How **industry, standards bodies, and government** coordinate transition timelines—consistent with **NIST IR 8547** deprecation guidance for legacy algorithms

The catalog supports not only federal CIO shops but also **critical infrastructure** owners and operators that follow CISA risk-management guidance on [post-quantum cryptography](https://www.cisa.gov/topics/risk-management/quantum).

## Practical procurement use

Security and sourcing teams can use the categories to:

1. **Attach PQC language to existing RFQs** without waiting for full stack rewrites  
2. **Score vendor responses** on standards support, hybrid modes, and crypto-agility roadmaps  
3. **Align shared services** (identity, WAN, email, code signing) so migrations do not stall on a single missing component category  

Agencies are also encouraged to pair category requirements with **automated cryptographic discovery** pilots—CISA and NIST’s **ACDI** work—to verify what algorithms actually run in production.

## QTL perspective

Quantum Transition Labs frequently finds **gaps between policy intent and shelf-ready products**—especially for **blockchain validators**, **custom HSM workflows**, and **telecom-integrated key storage**. CISA’s category framework is the right layer for **accountability**; QTL’s assessments close the gap between **category checklists** and **architecture-specific cutover plans**.
